### [5] [A Small-Ship Object Detection Method for Satellite Remote Sensing Data](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10574227/)
*Xiyu Fan,Zhuhua Hu,Yaochi Zhao,Junfei Chen,Tianjiao Wei,Zixun Huang*

Main category: Journal: IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations and Remote Sensing

Source: Journal; journal: IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations and Remote Sensing (Remote-sensing core tier); abstract: OpenAlex

Research relevance score: 9

TL;DR: CSDP-YOLO improves small-target ship detection in satellite remote sensing images via a CSDP feature-extraction module (deep large-kernel convolution + point convolution) and MPDIoU loss, achieving high AP/recall/AP50 on a private dataset and SSDD, but reports no efficiency or deployment metrics.

Research relevance: Directly relevant to remote-sensing satellite ship detection and small-target object detection (matched profile terms: remote sensing, object detection). However, relevance to the lightweighting/deployment dimension is limited because no efficiency metrics or deployment evidence are reported. Useful mainly for the small-target detection module and loss-function ideas, not for the efficiency side.

Reading priority: 3

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>

Task and scene: Task: small-target ship object detection in satellite remote sensing imagery. Scene: marine monitoring with complex interferences (clouds, islands, farmed nets). Sensor/modality: satellite optical remote sensing images (high contrast, low noise). Target class: ships (small targets).

Model architecture: CSDP-YOLO, a YOLO-based detector. Key innovation is the CSDP module: deep large-kernel convolution to enhance the receptive field of shallow features, plus point convolution to mix channel positions for better feature extraction. MPDIoU loss is introduced to address class imbalance between small ships and background.

Lightweight method: No explicit lightweighting method is reported. The paper focuses on detection accuracy (CSDP module and MPDIoU loss) rather than compression, pruning, quantization, distillation, or NAS. Abstract does not specify any efficiency-oriented design.

Onboard deployability: No deployment evidence reported. Abstract does not specify parameters, FLOPs, latency, memory, energy, or target hardware. The paper is accuracy-oriented; onboard feasibility is not addressed.

Datasets and metrics: Private dataset: 3881 remote-sensing ship images with 8418 ship instances across complex environments (clouds, islands, farmed nets). Public dataset: SSDD. Metrics: average precision (AP), recall, AP50. On the private dataset: AP 90.1%, recall 86.6%, AP50 91.4%. On SSDD: AP 93.6%, recall 93.7%, AP50 96.8% (stated as "highest" among compared methods).

Experiments: Comparison against "other state-of-the-art algorithms" on both the private dataset and SSDD. Results show CSDP-YOLO significantly improves small-target ship detection on the private dataset, and achieves the highest AP/recall/AP50 on SSDD. Abstract does not specify which baselines were compared, nor any ablation studies.

Limitations: Abstract does not specify model parameters, FLOPs, latency, memory, or energy consumption, and no deployment evidence (edge hardware, FPGA/NPU, onboard/in-orbit inference) is reported. Baselines and ablation details are not given. The private dataset is not publicly described in detail. No oriented-box or SAR/multispectral modality is mentioned.

Ideas for my research: 1) The CSDP module (deep large-kernel convolution to enlarge the receptive field of shallow features + point convolution for channel-position mixing) is a transferable idea for small-target detection in satellite imagery; you could evaluate whether the same module helps oriented-box or SAR ship detection. 2) MPDIoU loss for class-imbalance between small targets and background is directly reusable in your lightweight detector training pipeline. 3) Note the paper reports no efficiency metrics, so you could re-implement CSDP-YOLO and measure params/FLOPs/latency to see whether it is compatible with onboard constraints before adopting it.

Abstract: Satellite remote sensing technology can achieve real-time observation of ships at sea, and the remote sensing images obtained have the advantages of high contrast and low noise and have become one of the important means of marine monitoring. For the satellite remote sensing image data, there are two main problems: first, remote sensing data class-imbalance problem, and second the existing target detector in the presence of clouds, islands, farmed nets, and other interferences on the small-target ship, and there is a leakage of detection and wrong detection problem. To address the above problems, first, a new dataset containing 3881 images of remotely sensed ships in a variety of complex environments is constructed, which contains a total of 8418 ship instances. Second, we propose CSDP-YOLO for the small-target ship detection method with remote sensing data class imbalance. In order to enhance the performance of neural networks for small-target ship detection in remote sensing images, the innovative CSDP module is proposed, which uses deep large kernel convolution to enhance the sensory field of shallow features and mixes the channel positions using point convolution to obtain a more excellent feature extraction performance. Finally, the MPDIoU loss function is introduced to solve the class-imbalance problem between remote sensing small target ships and the background. We compare the performance with other state-of-the-art algorithms. The experimental results show that the proposed CSDP-YOLO algorithm can significantly improve the performance of small-target ship detection for private datasets. Its average precision, recall, and$\text{AP}_{50}$are improved to 90.1%, 86.6%, and 91.4%, respectively. For the SSDD public remote sensing dataset, its metrics can reach the highest 93.6%, 93.7%, and 96.8%, respectively.
